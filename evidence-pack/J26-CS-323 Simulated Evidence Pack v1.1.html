1. What this pack is, and what it is not

  • It is the test subject for our method: one simulated AI credit-scoring system, described the way a bank would document it, with the evidence an assessor would ask to see.
  • It is traceable: every evidence item states which public standard or regulation asks for it (Section 4), and each source was looked up on 21 October 2026 and its check status is shown in Section 8.
  • It is the single source of the test cases already built into the four tools (Section 5). The tools and this pack use the same codes.
  • It is not real data. Bank S does not exist. Findings such as “all 50 sampled outputs carry reason codes” describe an imagined evidence state, not a measurement.
  • It is not expert-validated yet. No expert judgements have been collected (Section 6). After PP1, bank practitioners will be asked whether the system and its evidence are realistic.

Why simulated? We assess the AI system, not its customers, so no customer data is needed. A real bank’s AI controls and weaknesses are confidential security information that a bank would not release to a student project.

2. The simulated system

2.1 System profile

AttributeValue (simulated)
Asset IDSIM-CREDIT-SCORING-01
Operator“Bank S” — a fictional mid-sized licensed commercial bank in Sri Lanka
Business useCredit decisions on unsecured retail personal loans
What the AI doesEstimates each applicant’s probability of default within 12 months and turns it into a recommendation: Approve, Refer to an officer, or Decline
Model typeGradient-boosted decision-tree classifier, retrained quarterly (in-house in variants V1–V2; vendor or third-party components in V3–V4)
Inputs42 features from the application, the bank’s own loan and account history, and a credit-bureau feed
ExplanationsPer-decision reason codes produced with SHAP (Shapley additive explanations)
Decision ownerHead of Credit Risk (accountable owner, 1st line)
Why it mattersCredit scoring of natural persons is listed as high-risk in the EU AI Act, Annex III point 5(b). Bank S is not in the EU, but this shows how regulators rate this use.

5. Test-case evidence states

A test case is one possible state of Bank S’s evidence, from well governed to weakly governed. The expected result of every test case was fixed before the tool computed it. All 25 test cases pass in the tools (re-run on 24 October 2026): the 18 shown here plus C4's 7 accountability tests, which check the owner routing rather than an evidence state.

Each component's test cases were built to test that component on its own. C1's TC1 and C3's TC1 are separate evidence states, not the same state of Bank S, so a document can be rated differently in each. One joint evidence state for all four components comes with integration.

7. Limitations

  • Bank S, its documents and every finding are invented. Results show that the method behaves as designed, not how any real bank performs.
  • The evidence is described in summary form. The underlying documents (the model card, the bias report and so on) do not exist as separate files yet.
  • Clause-level mappings show where a source asks for something similar. They are not legal advice and do not prove compliance.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 text has not been read (paid standard). Sri Lankan sources are listed as context and are not yet mapped clause by clause.
  • All weights are placeholders until the expert review (Section 6).
